# Datasheets, I/O boards and carrier revisions

Find the original device documentation and understand which connector a reference actually covers.

## Read the documentation status

Every listing shows board-datasheet, original-website and visual-reference status. Recorded means evidence has been attached; missing means more research or classification is needed. A legacy source URL is not a newly checked manufacturer page. All source links remain available in the expanded document list.



## Choose the right document

A board datasheet describes a specific physical product. A chip datasheet cannot establish the breakout board pin order, regulator limits or connector polarity. Hardware manuals, carrier specifications, schematics and physical pinouts are linked separately. Saved documents open in the guide; online links require internet access.



## Match the carrier as well as the module

Jetson and compute-module systems combine a processor module with a carrier board. A header diagram for one carrier does not establish the pinout of a third-party carrier. Thor expansion connectors differ from the Orin 40-pin headers. Match the printed PCB revision and connector designator before following a diagram.



## Know what a visual establishes

A photograph or labeled board layout helps identify a device and locate connectors. A schematic symbol or signal table may not show the physical mating orientation. Physical connector maps are labeled separately; partial references are not approval of every contact on a device.



## Inspect the gaps

The audit covers every listing, including undocumented and generic records, without claiming that every device or datasheet in existence has been found.
